How to check a token contract before you buy

Most token losses don't come from clever hacks. They come from powers the contract openly gives its owner: print more supply, block your wallet, raise the sell tax, pause transfers, or replace the code. Each of those can be checked in a few minutes. Here is what to look for and what it means.

1. Can the code be replaced? (proxy / upgradeable)

If the token is an upgradeable proxy, everything below can change tomorrow: whoever controls the upgrade can deploy new logic. On a block explorer, a proxy usually shows a Read as Proxy tab and an implementation address. The question is then who can upgrade: a single wallet, a multisig, or a timelock.

2. Who is the owner?

Call owner() (or look for an admin role). Then open that address:

Owner is…What it means
a regular wallet (EOA)One private key can use every power below, instantly. Highest risk.
a Safe multisigSeveral signers must agree (check the threshold, e.g. 3-of-5).
a timelockChanges are announced and delayed (check the delay; 24–48 h gives holders time to exit).
the zero addressOwnership renounced: owner-only functions are dead. Roles or a proxy admin can still hold power.

3. Can the owner mint?

Look for mint(address,uint256) or similar in the contract's write functions. Unlimited minting by one key means your share can be diluted to nothing.

4. Can the owner change the tax?

Functions like setFee, setSellFee, setTaxes or updateSellFees let the owner raise the sell tax, sometimes to 100%. This is the classic honeypot: you can buy, but selling returns nothing. Also check for a fee-exemption list (insiders trading tax-free).

5. Can the owner block your wallet?

blacklist, setBots, freeze, addToBlacklist: any of these lets the owner stop chosen addresses from transferring. Big regulated stablecoins have this too (it is expected there); for a fresh meme token it is a red flag.

6. Can transfers be paused — and are they paused now?

A pause() function can halt all transfers. Call paused(): if it returns true right now, you probably can't move the token.

7. Is there a trading switch or adjustable limits?

enableTrading / setTradingEnabled mean trading depends on the owner. setMaxTxAmount / setMaxWallet can be tightened until selling is effectively blocked.

8. Is the source verified?

Unverified code means you are trusting bytecode nobody can easily read. Check the explorer's “Contract” tab or Sourcify. Verified doesn't mean safe, but unverified plus any power above is a strong reason to walk away.

What none of this can tell you

These checks find declared powers. They don't simulate a sell, can miss renamed functions, and say nothing about liquidity locks or the team. A clean result is a starting point, not a guarantee.
